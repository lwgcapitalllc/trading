# Liquidity Blocks

The **mechanic** half of Aaron's model. It defines one object — the liquidity block — and
that object does exactly two jobs: it is the zone you enter from, and it is the level your
stop hides behind. Watch this before
[the continuation model](2026-08-13-continuation-model-engineered-liquidity.md); the model is
built on top of it and the presenter says so.

Different presenter from the continuation video (same channel).

## The definition

**A liquidity block is the swing extreme that a sweep left behind.**

Bearish LB, built in the diagram at [00:42-01:42]:

1. Market trending up — higher highs, higher lows.
2. Price prints a low, then sells off and **takes that low out**. Retail reads this as a
   bearish break of structure and sells. That level is the **trap** (drawn as a red box).
3. Price then stabs back **up and sweeps the high**.
4. That swept high — the area around it — **is the bearish liquidity block.**

Bullish LB is the exact inversion [02:02-03:05]: downtrend, a high gets taken out (retail
buys the "break"), price stabs down and sweeps the low, and that swept low is the bullish LB.

## Why the level holds — the actual logic

Stated at [01:42-02:02] and it is the only reasoning offered, so it carries the whole idea:

> *"Since we have swept out previous liquidity, now we view this high right here to hold no
> liquidity, meaning we don't have a reason to run above it."*

The high has already been raided. The stops that sat above it are gone. There is no fuel
left up there, so price has no errand above it — which is what makes it a **safe place to
hide a stop**, not a magic reversal level.

## The two jobs, and only two

**1. Entry zone.** Either treat the whole LB area as the entry [02:43-03:05], or drop to a
lower timeframe *inside* the LB and refine an entry there — his example: LB found on M15/H1,
refine on M1.

**2. Stop-loss reference — the more important one.** [05:07-05:27] and again [06:48-07:28]:

> *"All the liquidity block does for you essentially is provide you a level to place your
> stop loss. … No liquidity block, no stop loss."*

Bullish LB → stop below the LB's **low**. Bearish LB → stop above the LB's **high**. He is
blunt about why it is stated as a rule: [07:08] *"I see a lot of people getting greedy or
just placing it in random levels. No, you want to keep it as repeatable and systematic as
possible."*

⚠ **"No LB, no stop loss" is a NO-TRADE condition, not a formatting preference.** If there
is no liquidity block, there is no systematic level to hide behind, so there is no entry.

## The two warnings — these are the reason the video exists

🔴 **Liquidity is priority one; the LB is priority two.** [03:45-04:26] The strongest
statement in the video and it is a warning against the thing his own audience does:

> *"If you don't understand liquidity, all you're going to be doing is plotting on highs and
> lows that have been taken out, taking entries left, right and centre, and you are going to
> accumulate a lot of unnecessary losses."*

Direction and bias come from liquidity. The LB is only the execution detail once the story
already exists. He repeats it in the outro at [08:08] rather than leaving it as an aside.

⚠ **Not every LB is an entry LB.** [05:27-06:08] He marks several on the chart that he would
*not* trade, and this is deliberate teaching. Every LB is worth **identifying** — because it
tells you where a reaction is likely, and lets you anticipate a pullback rather than be
surprised by one — but only the ones aligned with the liquidity story are **traded**. He
demonstrates the difference four or five times on the chart between [04:26] and [07:48].

## Chart section

XAUUSD 15-minute, [03:25-08:08]. He deliberately refuses to discuss bias or direction here so

recognition, marks both tradeable and non-tradeable LBs, and shows one bullish entry with the

## What is worth acting on

- The LB is a **swept swing extreme**. Detecting it needs a sweep event, not a candle pattern.
  This is why it is not the same object as a classic order block.
- **Stop placement is fully determined** by the LB — a mechanical rule, not discretion.
- The LB is downstream of liquidity. Any encoding of this must gate on the liquidity read
  first, or it produces exactly the "entries left, right and centre" failure he names.
- "Not every LB is traded" means an LB detector will fire far more often than the strategy
  trades. Expect a low hit rate from detection alone; the filter is the story above it.
