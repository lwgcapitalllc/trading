# The Continuation Model — engineered liquidity

The **strategy** half. Uses the liquidity block from
[the LB video](2026-08-13-liquidity-blocks-the-entry-and-stop-mechanic.md) as its entry and
stop mechanic, and adds the thing that makes it a setup rather than a level: **the market has
to build the fuel for the move before you are allowed in.**

Presenter calls it *"a bread and butter kind of setup for myself"* [08:21].

Structure of the video: whiteboard diagram [00:42-10:03], then one AUDUSD example on H1/M15
with the replay tool [10:03-14:28]. Everything below is the bearish/short version, which is
how he teaches it. The long is the exact mirror.

## The setup, in order

**Step 1 — find liquidity, and prove it. This is the target.** [01:24-02:07]

A low is only "liquidity" once price has come *back* to it and **respected** it. His test:
market prints a low → goes bullish → returns to that low → holds it → goes bullish again.
That second respect is the confirmation that buy-side orders are resting under it. Mark the
level. **This is what the trade will target**, and it is marked before any entry exists.

**Step 2 — the market sells off, engineering liquidity as it goes.** [02:29-04:13]

Standard sequence: highs get swept, price runs up to induce buyers before each breakdown,
retail structural highs get taken and then rejected. Each pullback high left behind is more
resting liquidity above.

**Step 3 — 🔴 THE TRAP. Price trades into the lows from step 1 and reacts UP.** [04:13-05:16]

This is the pivot of the whole model and the part he says is most important. Price arrives at
the proven liquidity, does **not** take it, and moves away to the upside. He marks this
region as a **red box** and reads the up-move as **false**:

> *"If we respect lows to the left, this liquidity, and move away from it — massive red flag.
> The market just does not operate like this. We don't just respect liquidity and then
> continue all the way back up."* [06:37-06:58]

Untaken liquidity below + price walking away from it = the up-move is **engineering**, not a
reversal. The lows become the target rather than a floor.

**Step 4 — price grinds up, engineering more liquidity.** [06:16-07:18]

Each pullback high on the way up is another pool. He annotates them **"Eng. LQ"** on the
diagram. This step is the entry condition, not scenery — see the gate below.

**Step 5 — ENTRY: the moment the engineered high is run.** [07:18-08:41]

> *"As soon as this high is taken out, you now have the option of pressing sell."*

Two accepted entries:
- Wait for price to tap the last internal high above (the refined version), or
- **His preference:** sell anywhere inside the zone as soon as the engineered liquidity high
  is run. *"I don't bother with waiting."*

**Stop loss: above the high from the left — the liquidity block.** [07:18-07:39] Stated with
no flexibility: *"always. No ifs, ands or buts."*

**Target: the step 1 lows.** The engineered liquidity below. He is dismissive of the R:R on
the diagram and says to ignore it — it is drawn small to fit, not measured [07:39-08:01].

## 🔴 The gate — no engineering, no trade

[08:01-08:21], the sharpest rule in the video:

> *"Now, if this doesn't occur, there is no sell. We need to see the market engineer
> liquidity. Once we engineer, probabilities on this trade skyrocket."*

Step 4 is a **hard precondition**. A continuation without the engineering leg is not this
setup.

## ⚠ The two mistakes he spends the most time on

**1. Don't sell just because a high broke — check what is above it first.** [05:36-06:37]

The pattern trader sells the first taken high. But if there is liquidity resting *above* that
high, the seller is the one being trapped. His framing: *"Where's the logic in selling here?
Why are we selling here, if we have a level of liquidity right above?"* You wait until the
liquidity above has been **run**, and then you sell. Same reasoning as the LB video — a level
is only safe once it has been raided.

**2. 🔴 THE SHAKEOUT — do not roll your stop.** [09:02-10:03], then demonstrated live on the
chart at [13:28-14:08].

The expected sequence after entry, described *in advance* as normal:

1. Sharp reaction in your favour — you are in profit quickly.
2. Price rushes **back up**, induces higher once more, often printing a **new high**.
3. *Then* the real sell-off begins.

> *"This is where a lot of you guys get scared or shaken out. Do not be alarmed. As long as
> your stop loss stays above this high from the left, everything should be fine. … Do not
> immediately roll your stop above this high out of fear. We don't do that. No fear."*

On the AUDUSD replay he shows it explicitly: rolling the stop to the nearer high gets you
**taken out right before** the move that would have paid — then the trade melts to target.

⚠ **This directly contradicts a breakeven-on-first-favourable-move exit ladder.** The model
requires the original stop to survive an adverse excursion that the trade is *expected* to
make. Worth flagging against
[the A+ entry-bar breakeven defect](../../CLAUDE.md) — same class of problem, opposite
direction: there, breakeven promoted on a move the position never made; here, breakeven would
kill a trade the model says is healthy.

## The chart example — AUDUSD, H1 → M15

[10:03-14:28]. Worth re-watching as the worked reference.

- **H1** [10:03-11:04]: finds the low that was respected → liquidity below, currently unswept,
  price moving up away from it. Names it **engineered liquidity**. *"You want to see this low
  print. That's the first step."*
- **M15** [11:04-11:47]: liquidity building at the highs; **equal highs** to the left as a
  second pool; identifies the LB above that will hold the stop.
- **Live PA** [12:07-12:48]: price trades into the trap area, gives a **false reaction down**
  taking a low out — which induces *more* sellers at the highs — then turns up. LB above, and
  the H1 continuation story still intact below.
- **Execution** [12:48-13:08]: high taken → in. Stop above the high. Target the H1 lows.
- **The shakeout, then target** [13:28-14:08].

## What is worth acting on

The model decomposes into five testable conditions. Each is detectable; the third and fourth
are the ones that carry the edge:

1. A **proven** liquidity level — a low returned to and respected. (Not merely "a low".)
2. Price **reaches** that level's region and **fails to take it**, then moves away. → the trap.
3. That failure re-designates the level from support to **target**.
4. **Engineered liquidity** builds on the move away. ← hard gate, no engineering no trade.
5. Entry on the **run** of that engineered high; stop above the LB; target the step 1 level.

Open questions for encoding, none of them answered in the video:

- ⚠ **How close is "into the lows"?** He eyeballs the region. No tolerance is given, and the
  red trap box is drawn by hand. This needs a measured band, not a guess.
- ⚠ **How long does the setup stay valid?** No expiry is stated at all.
- ⚠ **What invalidates it before the stop?** He mentions *"unless the setup is just invalid
  overall"* [09:23] and never defines it.
- ⚠ **No numbers anywhere in this video.** No win rate, no sample, no backtest — the claims
  are *"extremely high strike rate"* and *"probabilities skyrocket"*, both unquantified. He
  tells the viewer to go and backtest it themselves [08:01-08:21]. Every figure this model
  ever produces here will have to be ours.
