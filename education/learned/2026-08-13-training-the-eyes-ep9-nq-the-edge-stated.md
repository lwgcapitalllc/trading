# Training The Eyes Ep. 9 — NQ 15m, the edge stated in one line

NQ, M15 with one drop to M5, replay. **The richest episode of the series after Ep. 5**, and the
only one that contains a number.

## 🔴 The edge, in his own words

[03:48]:

> *"Remember, **our edge in the market is to buy once buyers are taken out.**"*

That is the whole model in nine words. Everything else — LBs, traps, targets, sessions — is
apparatus around it.

## 🔴 "We do not predict. We let the market communicate."

[02:07-02:32], the epistemics of the whole method and the best-stated idea in nine videos:

> *"**We do not predict that there's going to be liquidity above this high. No, we simply let the
> market communicate that to us.** And what happened here? This high, if you look to the left,
> **respected** this high from the left. What does that tell us? We have built liquidity at this
> high. **And we didn't just guess.** We're not marking this high out, guessing… **we let the
> market communicate that for us. And that is very, very important.**"*

The rule: **a level only counts once price has come back and respected it.** An untested high is
not liquidity. This is the same test the very first continuation video used for its target (a low
returned to and respected) — now stated as a general epistemic principle rather than one setup
step.

## 🔴 When to stay OUT — the cleanest no-trade rule in the series

[04:14-05:31]:

> *"Ask yourself this. **Has buyers been taken out if we just stayed above that low? No, they
> have not.** I view all of this price action right here to be a trap. **You want to avoid
> trading this price action because we are still trading above lows.**"*
>
> *"This right here teaches you when to stay out of the market. An easy rule: identify where we
> are building liquidity. **Any moves, any longs above it should be a trap.** And I call that
> **engineered liquidity**. … **If price comes below, now I can look for a buy opportunity. And
> that perspective is so powerful because you'll stop caring about every little move that's going
> on in the market.**"*

Stated as a switch: while price is **above** the identified pool, everything is a trap and you
are flat. Once price goes **below** it, a buy becomes available. That is a fully mechanical gate,
and it is the most directly encodable statement of the model in any of the nine videos.

⚠ **And he says plainly it is not a prediction** [05:31]: *"If price comes below — **cuz it
doesn't have to. This is not 100%.** And this is something you guys need to understand."* Also
[05:56]: *"**not every liquidity block's going to work.** Nothing is 100% in any strategy out
there."* Worth recording: across nine videos of unquantified claims, this is the one place he
volunteers that the thing fails.

## 🔴 THE FIRST HARD NUMBER IN NINE VIDEOS

[09:20]. A minimum R below which he will not take a partial:

> *"In this example I would **not** be partialling at this high. Why? **Cuz it's only a 1 to
> 1.6.** So what I would do is as soon as this high is taken, [move to] my entry. That's fine.
> And then I'd hold to my actual full target being up at the highs."*

**1:1.6 is too small to bank at.** Below that threshold he moves to breakeven at the level
instead and holds for the real target.

⚠ **Note the tension with the no-rolling rule.** Eps. 1, 7 and 10 all say do not move the stop to
breakeven out of fear. Here he moves to entry deliberately — at a *level being taken*, not on a
favourable excursion. Those are different triggers and the distinction matters for any exit
ladder: **breakeven on a level being run, never breakeven on distance travelled.**

## 🔴 Two entries — "extreme" and "confirmation" — scaled in

[08:54-09:48], the first formal statement of a two-order structure. Both are visible on screen at
once as two buy tools:

| | Where | Stop |
|---|---|---|
| **Extreme entry** | Limit at the low itself | Below the LB |
| **Confirmation entry** | Off the M5, after the sweep confirms | Below the M5 LB |

*"A possible **scale-in** opportunity… you have the **extreme entry** being down here, and a
**confirmation-based entry** that's taken off the 5-minute chart is right here."*

Both were filled on this trade. This reconciles the apparent Ep. 4 / Ep. 6 tension: the extreme
IS taken, as one of two orders — Ep. 4's *"I don't need the bottom"* means don't make the extreme
your **only** entry, not don't use it at all.

## 🔴 Hourly candle close as a timing trigger

[07:38], new and specific:

> *"Look at my crosshair. It's **10:45 a.m.** What happens at **11:00 a.m.**? We get an **hourly
> closure**. Price is moving bearish through 10:00. **As soon as 11:00 opens up the new hourly
> candle, we stab out the previous hourly low. And now the bullish move starts occurring.**"*

The sweep landed on the open of a new H1 candle. Same family as Ep. 4's London-open and
stock-open observations: **sweeps cluster at time boundaries.**

## The timeframe rule, stated mechanically

[08:28], answering his most-asked question for the second time in the series:

> *"Everyone says, what timeframes do you use? Check this out. **If this price action is very
> squeezed up, very bunched, it's hard to understand what's going on — so go to a lower
> timeframe. That's all I do.**"*

## The trade

M15: a high respects a high to the left → liquidity above, evidenced. Then ~2 days of range;
a low respects a low to the left → liquidity below at **2551**. Price sells off but *does not*
take 2551 → everything above it is a trap, stay out. Eventually price goes below → LB below →
**extreme entry** at the low, stop below the LB. Drops to M5, finds a second LB at **25610** →
**confirmation entry**, stop below that LB. First target the highs (partial hit at **25745**),
then the higher-timeframe highs. *"Boom, just like that. Clockwork."*

## What is worth acting on

- 🔴 **"Buy once buyers are taken out"** — the edge in one sentence, and the thing any encoding
  has to reproduce.
- 🔴 **The above/below switch is fully mechanical**: identify the pool → while price is on the
  near side, refuse everything → when price crosses it, the setup arms. This maps cleanly onto a
  state machine and is the most implementable rule in the series.
- 🔴 **A level must be evidenced by a respect before it counts.** Untested extremes do not qualify.
  This is a real filter that will cut the candidate set substantially.
- 🔴 **1:1.6 minimum for a partial** — the only number in nine videos. Not a measurement, but it is
  his stated threshold and it is testable.
- ⚠ **Two entries, scaled.** Extreme + confirmation, each with its own LB stop.
- ⚠ **Breakeven trigger is a LEVEL being run, not distance travelled.** Gets the no-rolling rule
  and the move-to-entry rule to coexist without contradiction.
- ⚠ **Sweeps cluster at HTF candle opens.** Testable directly off the bar timestamps.
- ✅ He states the method is not 100% and that LBs fail. First acknowledgement of failure in the
  series — still no rate attached to it.
