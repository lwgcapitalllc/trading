# "10,000 Hours of Liquidity" — the model on the weekly

NQ, **weekly → daily → H4 → H1**, deliberately never dropping below M15. His summary video. Same
model as [the Da Vinci spec](../../docs/DAVINCI_MODEL_SPEC.md); this is the high-timeframe proof.

## 🔴 The entry checklist, stated as a checklist

[05:39], the only place he enumerates it:

> *"We have **liquidity to the upside**. We have a **reason for us to push higher**. We have a
> **buildup of buyers**. And we have a **liquidity block below**. **Everything checks the boxes.**
> We need to be taking an entry **once the liquidity is taken.**"*

Four conditions plus the trigger. Maps onto the spec's sequence and is worth keeping as the
human-readable form.

## 🔴 THE BREAKEVEN TRIGGER — this resolves spec open question 9

The spec flags that the two Chart Fanatics videos **contradict each other on trade management**.
This video states the rule unambiguously, and four other videos in this folder agree:

> [06:41] *"The easiest way to manage this, very easy, very simple: **as soon as we take out this
> high, we can all agree that liquidity has been taken. So manage your risk** — maybe roll it to
> breakeven or just tuck it below this low. **When these highs are taken out, you need to be
> managing your position.**"*

**The trigger is a LEVEL being taken, never distance travelled.** That is what reconciles it with
the four separate no-rolling warnings (Training The Eyes Eps. 1, 7, 9, 10) — those all warn
against moving the stop *on a favourable excursion*. Moving it *when a liquidity level is
consumed* is a different event and is the actual rule.

Ep. 11 gives the same rule in negative form: *"We're only ever going to touch the stop loss once
the trade starts moving in the intended direction **and a good bit as well, not just a couple
pips**."*

## 🔴 LB refinement — how the RR is bought

[08:14], the mechanism behind the spec's "liquidity-block veto":

> *"The only issue with this liquidity block is **look how big it is.** We would have to enter
> here and our stop loss would have to cover the whole low. … We have **another liquidity block, a
> refined area** right here. So we can use that as a level to put our stop loss. **So that shrinks
> our SL quite a bit.**"*

So a fat LB is not always a veto — first drop a frame and look for a smaller LB inside it. Only if
that fails is the trade skipped.
