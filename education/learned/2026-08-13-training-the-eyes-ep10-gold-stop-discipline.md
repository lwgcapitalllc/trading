# Training The Eyes Ep. 10 — Gold H1/15m/5m

Shortest of the series. Gold, H1 down to M5, one long. Almost entirely repetition of the model —
its value is the **stop rule**, stated more forcefully here than anywhere else, and one more
session/volume observation.

⚠ Frames not extracted — see the note in the Ep. 7 file for why.

## 🔴 The stop rule, at maximum force

[05:07]:

> *"This is when we want to buy. **Stop-loss below the low to the left. I can't stress how
> important this is. It needs to be below LBs and above LBs. Trust these highs and trust these
> lows.**"*

And [06:24]: *"That is where we are entering positions. **Stop loss below the liquidity block
always.**"*

"Trust these highs and trust these lows" is the compressed form of the whole LB argument: the
level has been raided, so nothing needs to go back for it, so it is the one place a stop is safe.

## The no-rolling rule, fourth statement in the series

[04:17], and it names the emotion rather than the mechanic this time:

> *"Do not get excited. Look how the market rapidly moves up here. **A lot of people are going to
> roll their stop losses too early right below this low. And you're doing that based off emotion,
> forcing a break even, forcing a trade management.** Understand that if we get this move up and
> take out these highs, **now the market can easily come back down to this low.**"*

Note the reasoning: taking the highs *creates* the reason for price to return to the low. The
adverse excursion is caused by the favourable one. Fourth independent statement (Eps. 1, 7, 9,
10) that the original stop must survive it.

## Session + speed + volume, again

[02:34]: *"Look at this move that happened **right at New York open. Two 15-minute candles. Big
massive move.** The intention behind this move is to induce early buyers."*

[05:58], on M5: *"Look at this 5-minute candle coming down. This is going to induce so many

live time — retail traders analysing these charts are going to get induced to sell once this

Third video (with Eps. 6 and 4) to pair **a session boundary + few candles + high volume** as the
induction signature.

## Price reaches the LB extreme

Supports the Ep. 9 extreme entry — the extreme does get filled often enough to rest an order

## The trade

H1: big high-to-low sell-off induces sellers → red box at the high = trap. Price returns to the
highs, respected repeatedly → liquidity building above; **4353.5** marked as the target. M15:
a New York-open two-candle move induces early buyers → those buyers must be trapped before a long
is valid. Price sells off into the **extreme of the LB**, trapping them → **buy, stop below the
LB low**, target the highs. M5 manages: a high-volume down candle induces sellers, red box at the
high, entry on the reaction. *"Boom, clockwork."*

## What is worth acting on

Nothing structurally new. Its contribution is as **corroboration**, which is worth something on
its own:

- The stop rule now has its strongest statement — *"below LBs and above LBs, always."*
- The no-rolling rule is at four independent statements across the series. Whatever exit ladder we
  build for this model, **breakeven-on-favourable-excursion is ruled out**, and this is now
  overwhelmingly evidenced rather than inferred from one video.
- Session + few candles + high volume as the induction signature is at three statements.
