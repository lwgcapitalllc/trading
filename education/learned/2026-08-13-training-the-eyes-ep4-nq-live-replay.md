## What this video adds

### 1. 🔴 Session time is part of the setup, not background

He marks four times on the chart and treats them as structure:

| Time (NY) | What it is | What he expects |
|---|---|---|
| 03:00 | London open | The sweep. Liquidity builds *pre*-open, then price stabs through it right at the open |
| ~08:00 | New York open | Where he starts looking |
| 08:30 | News release | Liquidity builds before it; the move comes after |
| 09:30 | Stock open | The delivery — his target was hit in four M1 candles right at the bell |

[02:43] *"Both relevant times for us. Both important times for us."* The London open example at
[03:03] is the cleanest: *"literally right at London open, price builds all this liquidity
pre-London open and then right at London open, stabs below, grabs that liquidity. Anybody
buying, trapped."*

### 2. 🔴 The news rule — flat before, position after

[05:27], stated as absolute:

> *"I will never take a position before a news event. I'm always going to wait for the news
> event to occur and then capitalize afterwards. Because typically what happens before these
> news events is we build liquidity."*

Note the reasoning: news is not treated as a risk to dodge, it is treated as **the engine that
builds the liquidity the setup needs**. The sequence he shows is build → release → sweep →
entry.

### 3. The "from absolutely nothing" test

The sharpest new heuristic, and he uses it twice in both directions:

- [04:04] Price goes long at 07:55. *"What do we go long from? Absolutely nothing."* → therefore
  every long in that area is a false reaction. He marks the zone as random on purpose.
- [10:17] Price sells off. *"Where did we just go short from? Absolutely nothing. We've been
  building liquidity at this high forever, once, twice, three times. Now we get that false
  sell-off. **This is an automatic buy back up.**"*

The test: **a move that originates from no identified level is a false move**, and it is
tradeable in the opposite direction. This is the trap read from the continuation video stated
as a general rule instead of one setup step.

### 4. A level respected repeatedly is a stronger level — and it is counted

[01:41] He names one high — **23552** — and tracks it across days: *"Once, two times, and now
three times on this current day, we have now tapped into this area. We've been respecting it
over and over and over."* That high survives the timeframe changes and becomes the **final
partial target** at [08:31] because it is *"that final liquidity point on more of the higher
timeframe perspective."*

⚠ **The touch count is treated as strength.** The continuation video only required one
respect. Here three taps make it the runner target rather than the first target.

### 5. 🔴 Entry — "I don't need the bottom"

The most practically useful thing in the video, [06:08] and again [07:50-08:10]:

> *"I do not need the bottom. Price can come down and tap this candle, fill at the very bottom,
> or it can come to this one minute imbalance. That's fine. However, I do not need the bottom.
> I understand that price can go long anywhere in this area — essentially anywhere below this
> black line, the liquidity."*

And the warning attached to it:

> *"You could have waited for that extreme and that would have worked out for you. However, it's
> not always going to occur. This is going to be the better entry here. I want you guys to be
> taking these kind of entries, not this, cuz you're going to be missing a lot of trades. **You
> don't need the bottom. You need the correct direction. That's what pays the profits.**"*

So the entry is a **zone, not a price** — anywhere past the swept level. Chasing the extreme is
named as a losing habit. Imbalances (FVGs) inside the zone are mentioned as optional refinement,
not as a requirement.

### 6. Targets — a defined ladder, which the earlier video did not give

[06:28-08:52]:

1. **First target is always the structural high** — *"Why? Because this is now where seller
   liquidity is going to be."* He is explicit that this is non-negotiable and explains it as
   paying yourself before a likely reaction: he expects pullbacks there whether or not the trend
   continues.
2. **Partials run to the higher-timeframe level** — the 23552 high.

He explicitly rejects the nearer pools: *"I'm not going to just target something over here
because that's not big enough for myself."* Result on this trade: **just over 1:3** [08:52].

### 7. The struggle at the level is expected

Twice: [09:55] price takes 15-25 minutes to complete the sweep, and [10:37] price struggles in
the red box ~45-50 minutes before the move. Same message as the shakeout in the continuation
video — **slow and ugly at the level is normal, not invalidation**. He also notes at [07:09] he
sized the trade knowing stock open was 30 minutes out and price could *"screw around"* until
then.

- The model is symmetric. Long and short are the same rules inverted, shown rather than claimed.
- The model is fractal — H1/M15 on AUDUSD, M15/M5/M1 on NQ, identical logic.
- The gate is restated in the strongest terms yet: no entry until the level is swept.

**New, and none of it is in the other two videos:**
- ⚠ **Session times are part of the setup.** London open, NY open, 09:30 stock open. We have
  `engines/sessions/` for exactly this and it is already DST-aware.
- ⚠ **News: flat before, position after.** We have `engines/news/` — and note its standing
  caveat that it is inert before its cache's earliest date, which would silently disable this
  rule on older history rather than fail loudly.

  wrong shape for this model — he names it as the reason people miss trades. That is a real
  difference from A+ and B-LEG, which rest limits.
- ⚠ **Touch count on a level is treated as strength** and decides which target it becomes. Not
  measured, not defined — how many taps, over what window, at what tolerance.
- ⚠ **Target ladder: structural high first, HTF liquidity on partials.** This is the first time
  a target rule has been given at all.
