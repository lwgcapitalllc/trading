# Training The Eyes Ep. 11 — AUDUSD H1, marking the wrong low

AUDUSD, H4 → H1 → M15. One long, full target. Its contribution is a **failure mode**: what happens
when you identify the level incorrectly.

## 🔴 Marking the wrong low is the failure mode

[03:52], and he says outright this is the fix for most viewers' problems:

> *"**If you didn't identify this — and this is going to clear a lot of issues up** — if you didn't
> mark on this low, and maybe you grabbed, I don't know, this low down here, **you're going to
> start getting induced into this move. If you didn't identify the liquidity properly**… Would you
> guys want to be a buyer below **this** one or **this** one?"*

The answer is the low with the **buyers' stops beneath it** — the one under the area where retail
stepped in after the BOS. [04:19]: *"You have buyers stepping in this area and then you have this
low down here where they are going to be placing their stop loss."*

And the rule that follows [04:45]:

> *"We want to be buying once **all** the liquidity is taken. **These lows getting taken right
> here is not all the liquidity taken.**"*

⚠ **"All the liquidity" is the criterion, not "a low was swept."** Marking a shallower low means
entering while a deeper pool is still unclaimed — which is precisely the induced move you were
trying to fade. This is the same defect as Ep. 8's left-side internal liquidity, seen from the
level-selection end instead of the sequencing end.

## Entry, restated once more

[05:37]: *"**We don't need the imbalance. We don't need the exact bottom. None of that stuff. We
want to enter as soon as the liquidity is taken**, which is basically this low from the left-hand
side. Entry. Stop loss, as you guys know, **always below the low to the left.**"*

Fourth consistent statement of the entry across the folder, and it matches Marco's "don't
over-refine" in the Chart Fanatics episode.

## 🔴 When the stop may be moved — the negative form of the rule

[06:27], the clearest phrasing of the breakeven trigger's *threshold*:

> *"Remember, stop loss always below this low. This doesn't change. **We're only ever going to
> touch the stop loss once the trade starts moving in the intended direction — and like a good bit
> as well, not just a couple pips.**"*

And [06:53], the reasoning for going risk-free once a partial lands:

> *"This is definitely, like, you want to be risk-free. Because you've got to think about it like
> this: **do you want price all the way back down to your entry and do you still want to be in that
> trade? I hope the answer is no.**"*

Consistent with the level-based trigger established in
[the 10,000 Hours note](2026-08-13-10000-hours-of-liquidity-htf-only.md) — he moves the stop after
the first target/level is reached, never on a couple of pips of favourable movement.

## R and target handling

**Full target ≈ 1:11.** [07:20] *"These kind of trades don't come around too often. But when they
do, you guys need to be taking advantage of these."*

Targets are discretionary between the pools: *"if you want to partial on the way up due to some
comfortability… that's completely fine. Do what you're comfortable with. **Manage the trade
according to your own comfortability.**" ⚠ The *levels* are rule-based; **which** one you take is
explicitly personal. That is a real gap for anything automated — it means the exit ladder is
underspecified by the source and will have to be our decision, measured.

## The target was the opposing traders' stops

[07:46], closing the loop on what a target *is*:

> *"We have ran for the full target back up at the highs **where sellers were entering from**. If
> they're entering positions up here, where's their stop loss going to be? **Tucked right above the
> high. And would you look at that — our buy hunted that liquidity perfectly.**"*

## Other confirmations

- **Round number** used as a level: 0.67 flat, *"the whole number"*.
- **Touch counting**: *"once, twice, three times, four times"* on a 15-minute low.
- **Sessions**: the build printed in **Asia** (~12:45-1:00 a.m.); the induction move came at
  **New York open**.
- **BOS as generator**: *"That is a BOS for retail… every single time we take out another high, we
  are simply inducing more buyers. **But we know that we need to wait for this low to get taken
  before we want to enter.**"*

## What is worth acting on

- 🔴 **Level selection is the failure mode, not sequencing.** The rule is *all* the liquidity, and
  the correct low is the one under where the induced traders' stops sit — which means the detector
  has to pick the **deepest unclaimed** pool, not the nearest swept one.
- 🔴 **Stop moves only after a meaningful move / a level taken — "not just a couple pips."**
- ⚠ **Which target to take is explicitly discretionary in the source.** Any ladder we build is our
  invention and must be measured as such, not attributed to the model.
