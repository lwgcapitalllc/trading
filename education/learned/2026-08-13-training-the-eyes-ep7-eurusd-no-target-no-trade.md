# Training The Eyes Ep. 7 — EURUSD, "no target, no trade"

EURUSD, lower timeframes only, replay. Deliberately single-frame: *"We're not going to be going
from higher timeframe to lower timeframe. That is for other videos."*

⚠ **Frames not extracted for this one.** By this point five videos of the same chart grammar had
been read frame-by-frame (red box = trap, blue line = liquidity, LB = the swept extreme) and this
episode's transcript is unusually explicit. A deliberate token trade-off, recorded so nobody
later reads this note as having the same evidence base as Eps. 4-6.

## 🔴 The new gate: NO TARGET, NO TRADE

The most important thing in the episode, and it is a genuinely new condition. [01:16-01:42]:

> *"This induces buyers into the market, which means we could probably see a short back down.
> **However — do we have liquidity below the low?** That's the real question for you guys. **I
> don't see any liquidity down here yet. So there's no reason for us to target this yet. So
> there's no reason for us to look for that short yet.**"*

And again at [03:00]: *"I'm literally going to need more price action, because **if I don't see
liquidity other than these lows, then we have no reason to trade yet.**"*

He then plays price forward and *waits* until the target exists.

⚠ **This inverts the usual order of a setup.** The trade is not "find an entry, then pick a
target." The **target must already exist and be evidenced** before the setup is even a candidate.
Ep. 5's *"the most important part is we have liquidity to target"* said the same thing; here it
is enforced live, with him refusing to look for the trade at all.

## Two valid entries, both with the same stop

[07:39-09:24] he offers a pair rather than one:

**Entry 1 — immediate.** The moment the high from the left is taken. *"You could have entered
right away and that's fine."*

**Entry 2 — after the re-trap.** If price then induces and traps *once more* on a lower
timeframe, that second sweep is another entry. *"Another opportunity was as soon as that high is
taken out."*

**Both use the same stop: above the high from the left.** Not above the nearer high the second
sweep created.

## The shakeout, explained through the stop-rolling mechanic

[08:04-08:56] — third video to teach this, and the clearest on *why* it happens:

> *"Let's say you enter as soon as this high gets taken out with your stop above this high. When
> you get that selloff you're a little bit in profit, and what you're probably going to do is
> **roll your stop above this high**. And look what price does — comes back up and takes it out.
> Takes that trader out who mismanaged their position… **You've now left an option for price to
> come up and take you out and then run to the downside.**"*
>
> *"If you enter right away, what happens sometimes is we induce and then trap once more and then
> the move occurs. Don't get psyched out… **Don't mismanage your trade based off emotion.**"*

## Where the liquidity comes from — sellers rolling their own stops

[04:42], a mechanism not spelled out in the earlier videos:

> *"If sellers entered here and here, their stop loss is going to be right above this high. **If
> it was maybe above this high, as soon as this big sell-off occurs, they're probably rolling
> their stop loss above this high or maybe even to break even.**"*

So the pool above a level is fed twice: by the original stops, and by traders in profit
*tightening* stops into the same place. The model's own no-roll rule is the mirror of this —
he refuses to add himself to the pool he is hunting.

## Other confirmations

- Retail BOS as generator, again [01:42]: *"we can mark this down as a BOS because that's where
  retail would be marking this out, and they want to be buying back down at the low."*
- A level is evidenced by a **respect**: *"This high respected this high… tapped into it and
  moved away."* Two respects then a sell-off = liquidity above.
- The trigger leg named yet again [09:24]: *"This bullish move here **from low to high. This is
  false.** This is just inducing buyers."*
- LB definition unchanged: *"We grab a level of liquidity and we move away."*

## What is worth acting on

- 🔴 **"No target, no trade" is a hard precondition** and is new. Encoding order becomes: find the
  target pool first → then wait for the sweep → then entry. A scanner built the other way round
  will fire on setups he would refuse.
- ⚠ **Two entries per setup, one stop.** Anything that models this needs to allow a second fill on
  a re-trap without moving the stop — which is the opposite of a trailing ladder.
- ⚠ The stop-rolling mechanism explains *why* the shakeout is so reliable, and it is the third
  independent statement of the no-breakeven rule across the series.
