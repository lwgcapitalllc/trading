# Training The Eyes Ep. 13 — NQ 1-minute, three identical entries

NQ on the **1-minute**, replay mode. The lowest frame in the series (Ep. 12 is the highest, the
daily). Its contribution is the clearest demonstration that the model **repeats within a single
move**, plus a second set of R figures.

## 🔴 Three entries, structurally identical, in one leg

[06:51-07:44]. He marks three separate short entries on the same move and shows they are the same
object:

> *"Possible entry number one… possible entry number two… possible entry number three. **Look at
> the similarities between all of these entries.** Entry here. Why? **Look to the left. LB.**
> Entry number two here. Why? **You have your LB.** Entry number three here. Why? **Here's your
> LB.** So all three of these entries — **repeatable, the exact same thing over and over and
> over. Build, entry. Build, entry.**"*
>
> *"And every single time, look what we were doing at the lows. **We were building liquidity at
> the low. So there's your logic, there's your reasoning for price to move lower.**"*

This is the Ep. 5 cycle running three times inside one directional leg, each iteration a complete
build → sweep → LB → entry, all sharing one target pool. It is why the model can produce several
positions from one move rather than one.

## 🔴 The R figures

[08:10-08:36], the second set of numbers in the series after Ep. 9's 1:1.6 threshold:

| Entry | To the full low | To a nearer low |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | over **1:4** | **1:3** |
| 2 | over **1:8** | **1:5.7** |
| 3 | over **1:8** | **1:5.2** |

⚠ These are drawn-on-the-chart measurements of one move in hindsight, not a track record. They
are worth recording only as an indication of the R **shape** he is aiming at — the later entries
in a sequence carry the better ratio because the stop tightens while the target stays fixed.

## 🔴 The fractal claim, stated outright

[06:01], and this settles it:

> *"This is the one-minute chart, so it might be hard for you guys to understand, but **this price
> action is actually the exact same as this price action here. It's just squeezed up a little
> bit.**"*

He then shows the M1 sub-pattern is the same object as the higher-frame one — *"So it's literally
the exact same as this."* Same mechanical rule as Eps. 5 and 9: if it's squeezed, drop a frame;
the object does not change.

## Confirmation entry when you aren't confident

[03:00]: *"**If you're not confident in taking this entry right here, all you have to do is simply
wait.** Wait for another entry, to build some sort of a **confirmation-based entry**."*

Same two-tier structure as Ep. 9 (extreme vs confirmation), here framed as a confidence dial
rather than a scale-in.

## Time-building, again

[01:18]: *"for around roughly **an hour and a half**, look what price does down here — we build in
this channel, chop around… **we are building liquidity down here.** And then price ever so
slightly takes the lows out, and from there we move bullish."*

Fifth instance of duration-as-magnitude across the series (5½ h, 11-12 h, 12-13 h, ~2 days, 1½ h).
Note this one is the *smallest* and it is on the M1 — the duration scales with the frame, which is
what a fractal rule requires and is a useful hint that the threshold should be measured in **bars,
not hours**.

## Everything else is unchanged

Sellers induced by a low being taken → their stops sit above the high → wait for that high to be
taken → enter → *"we tuck our stop losses above this high, which is known as our liquidity
block."* Targets are evidenced pools. Reactions anticipated so they don't surprise you.

## What is worth acting on

- 🔴 **The cycle repeats inside one leg.** Three entries, one target, each with its own tighter LB
  stop. Any encoding that assumes one position per move will under-count this model badly — and
  note the repo's standing lesson from Run 12: with **one position slot**, extra setups *queue in
  front of* each other rather than adding. This model explicitly wants several concurrent or
  sequential entries on one thesis, which is a different position-management shape from A+.
- 🔴 **Duration should be measured in BARS, not hours.** 1½ hours on M1 and 12 hours on H1 are the
  same signal at different scales. An hours-based threshold would silently only ever fire on high
  timeframes.
- ⚠ **R shape**: later entries in a sequence carry higher R because the stop tightens toward a
  fixed target. Worth testing whether entry 3 alone beats taking all three.
- ✅ **M1 → D1 demonstrated** across this episode and Ep. 12.
